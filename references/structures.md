# Structural patterns to avoid

## The compliment sandwich
Praise, then the real message wrapped in softeners, then more praise. The reader remembers the praise and misses the message.
Fix: lead with the main message. Give praise and criticism as separate, specific points.

## The verdict with no evidence
"Your communication needs work." A conclusion with no scene attached.
Fix: put the scene first (when, where, what happened), then the conclusion if still needed.

## The trait statement
"You are disorganized." Traits feel permanent and invite defense.
Fix: describe the behavior and its effect. "The last three planning docs arrived after the meeting started."

## The pile-on
Six issues in one review so none of them land.
Fix: pick the one or two that matter most for the next cycle. Park the rest.

## The borrowed voice
"People have said," "some on the team feel," "I have heard." Anonymous claims cannot be checked or acted on.
Fix: name the source if it is safe to, or state it as your own observation. If you cannot do either, leave it out.

## The unmeasurable ask
"Be more proactive," "show more leadership."
Fix: name an observable behavior and a window. "Raise a blocker in standup the day it appears, for the next two sprints."

## The silent comparison
"Below peers," "not at level." The reader cannot see the bar.
Fix: quote the expectation for the level, then describe the gap against it.

## The missing next step
Feedback that ends on the problem.
Fix: end with something the reader can do this week, and a moment when you will look at it together.

## Praise that cannot be repeated
"Great presentation." The person does not know which part to do again.
Fix: name the move that worked ("you opened with the customer's wait time") so they can reuse it.
